# GLP-1 / Incretin Technology: State of Play and 12-Month Strategy

## Decision summary

1.  **The core incretin drug market is dominated by two incumbents (Novo Nordisk, Eli Lilly) with massive capital commitments, making direct entry into drug manufacturing or novel receptor agonist discovery unviable for a mid-sized European company in the next 12 months.** The evidence shows Novo Nordisk planning a $4.1 billion facility and Lilly committing over $15 billion to new plants, while the patent innovation chain is measured as "slow-moving" with a median improvement rate of ~3.1%/yr [Measured innovation-chain profile: GLP-1 and incretin technology](https://catandary.de/trends/foresight/technology?q=GLP-1%20and%20incretin%20technology), [GLP-1 Drug Manufacturing: Expansion & Tech Transfer | IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/glp-1-drug-manufacturing-cdmo-tech-transfer).

2.  **The viable 12-month opportunity lies in the "GLP-1-adjacent" ecosystem: nutritional support for side effects (specifically muscle loss and GI issues) and regulatory-compliant health claims.** Evidence supports this via new patent filings for combinations with myostatin/ActRII modulators to preserve lean mass  and funding signals for metabolic health clinics and functional ingredients (e.g., Nourish’s $100M round) [Nourish Secures $100M for Metabolic Health Clinic](https://medcitynews.com/2026/05/nourish-secures-100m-for-metabolic-health-clinic/), [Rising Demand for Functional Ingredients Drives Innovation in the Food Sector](https://catandary.de/trends/rising-demand-for-functional-ingredients-drives-innovation-in-the-food-sector).

3.  **European regulatory barriers for "GLP-1 friendly" food claims are high but create a moat; any product must meet strict EFSA substantiation requirements for health-related claims.** The EU register and EFSA guidelines mandate scientific proof for claims linking food to disease risk reduction or physiological function, meaning "GLP-1 supportive" wording is not permitted without robust clinical evidence [Health claims (art. 13) - EFSA - Europa.eu](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/health-claims-art-13), [EU register of health claims - Food Safety - European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims/eu-register-health-claims_en), [GLP-1 Agonist Regulatory Guide for New Manufacturers](https://resource.ddregpharma.com/blogs/glp-1-agonist-regulatory-landscape-what-manufacturers-must-know-before-entering-new-markets/).

## What is moving

The GLP-1/incretin sector is in a phase of high commercial velocity despite a measured "slow-moving" patent innovation chain. The Catandary Foresight engine measures the technology improvement rate at a median of ~3.1%/yr over the measured history, with a cycle time of 11.0 years [Measured innovation-chain profile: GLP-1 and incretin technology](https://catandary.de/trends/foresight/technology?q=GLP-1%20and%20incretin%20technology). However, market signals show exponential growth: North America held 77.72% of worldwide sales in 2024, while Asia-Pacific is projected to grow at a CAGR of 14.6% through 2035 .

The primary movers are Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly. Novo Nordisk is expanding capacity with a $4.1 billion manufacturing facility in North Carolina, expected to open around 2029, and acquired Catalent for $16.5 billion to ramp production [GLP-1 Drug Manufacturing: Expansion & Tech Transfer | IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/glp-1-drug-manufacturing-cdmo-tech-transfer). Eli Lilly has committed over $15 billion to new plants in the US [GLP-1 Drug Manufacturing: Expansion & Tech Transfer | IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/glp-1-drug-manufacturing-cdmo-tech-transfer). A significant competitive shift is the move to oral small molecules. Eli Lilly’s orforglipron achieved FDA approval for chronic weight management in late 2025/2026, with Phase 3 data showing 12.4% weight loss, though it trails injectable rivals like tirzepatide in efficacy [FDA approves first GLP-1 pill for obesity from Wegovy maker Novo Nordisk](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/22/fda-approves-first-glp-1-pill-for-obesity-from-novo-nordisk.html), [Lilly’s oral GLP-1 drug orforglipron achieves 12.4% weight loss in phase 3 trial but trails injectable rivals tirzepatide and retatrutide](https://www.drugdiscoverytrends.com/lillys-oral-glp-1-drug-orforglipron-achieves-12-4-weight-loss-in-phase-3-trial-but-trails-injectable-rivals-tirzepatide-and-retatrutide/), [Eli Lilly's Pill Orforglipron Challenges the Injection Pen in Phase II Weight-Loss Trial](https://catandary.de/trends/the-oral-revolution-rethinking-glp-1-delivery-models-50824).

In Europe, the market is driven by private demand due to restrictive reimbursement. Germany only covers Wegovy if obesity poses a serious cardiovascular risk [The slimming shot: What price are you willing to pay for a dream body? | Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/health/2025/06/04/the-slimming-shot-what-price-will-europeans-pay-for-a-dream-body). Italy’s private spending on GLP-1 agonists doubled from €52 million in 2023 to over €115 million in 2024, driven by off-label use [The slimming shot: What price are you willing to pay for a dream body? | Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/health/2025/06/04/the-slimming-shot-what-price-will-europeans-pay-for-a-dream-body). Greece saw an 82.5% surge in anti-obesity drug usage in 2024 [The slimming shot: What price are you willing to pay for a dream body? | Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/health/2025/06/04/the-slimming-shot-what-price-will-europeans-pay-for-a-dream-body).

A critical scientific development is the recognition that GLP-1-induced weight loss includes significant muscle loss. This has triggered new patent filings for combinations with myostatin and ActRII pathway modulators to preserve lean mass . Additionally, next-generation molecules are expanding to dual (GIP/GLP-1) and triple (GLP-1/GIP/Glucagon) agonists, with companies like Zealand Pharma (raising $1 billion in 2024) and Septerna (signing a $2.2B deal with Novo Nordisk in 2025) securing significant funding , .

**Permitted Product Claims:**
In the EU, health claims on food products are strictly regulated under Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006. Claims that a food product is "GLP-1 friendly" or aids in weight loss are considered health-related claims and require scientific substantiation approved by the European Commission [Health claims (art. 13) - EFSA - Europa.eu](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/health-claims-art-13), [EU register of health claims - Food Safety - European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims/eu-register-health-claims_en). The EU Register lists authorised health claims; claims not in the register are not permitted [EU register of health claims - Food Safety - European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims/eu-register-health-claims_en). For example, a claim that a high-protein food "preserves muscle mass during GLP-1 therapy" would require specific clinical evidence and authorisation. Wording that implies the food treats or prevents a disease (e.g., "treats obesity") is not permitted on food labels [Health claims (art. 13) - EFSA - Europa.eu](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/health-claims-art-13). The sweep found no specific "GLP-1" claim currently authorised in the EU Register for food products; thus, such wording is not permitted today without a new application and approval.

### Option 1 — Develop High-Protein "Muscle Preservation" Nutrition
- **Trigger:** Clinical data confirming significant muscle loss in GLP-1 users and the launch of next-generation therapies that do not address this side effect.
- **Time horizon:** Product development and regulatory filing within 12 months; market launch in 18–24 months.
- **Effort:** Moderate. Requires formulation development and, critically, clinical trials to substantiate health claims under EFSA guidelines. No figure in the evidence for specific trial costs, but EFSA compliance is a high barrier.
- **Risk:** Regulatory rejection of health claims if clinical evidence is insufficient. Competition from pharmaceutical companies developing combination drugs (e.g., with myostatin modulators) that address muscle loss directly .
- **Against it:** The evidence shows that pharmaceutical companies are already filing patents for drug combinations that preserve lean mass . A food product may be rendered obsolete if a next-generation drug solves the muscle loss problem pharmacologically.

- **Time horizon:** R&D and supplier qualification within 12 months.
