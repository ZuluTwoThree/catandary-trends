## What the evidence does not support

The growth is commercialization-driven, not science-driven. The two readings coexist: the market is growing rapidly while the patent innovation rate is low. This is a phase-transition signal, not a contradiction in the data.

These are not directly comparable (different scope definitions), but both are secondary market-research estimates, not primary data. The corpus does not carry a peer-reviewed or regulatory confirmation of this growth rate.

- **Claim: "47–58% of patients discontinue or regain weight."** Amgen's patient-facing page states 47% with T2DM discontinued within 1 year and 58% regained weight within 12 months of discontinuation [The Gap Between GLP-1 Prescriptions and Persistence — Amgen](https://www.amgen.com/obesity/prescription-gap). However, a Cleveland Clinic analysis of nearly 8,000 patients found that discontinuing semaglutide and tirzepatide "on average, does not lead to significant weight regain in clinical practice, as many patients later restart the original medication". These two readings contradict each other on a checkable fact. The Amgen figure comes from EHR claims data; the Cleveland Clinic finding is observational and may reflect re-initiation patterns. Both are in the corpus; neither is definitive.

- **Claim: "A mid-sized European company should enter the companion nutrition market."** This is an inference, not a finding. The recommendation is directionally supported but financially unquantified.

- **Claim: "France's June 2026 reimbursement will expand the European patient pool."** The reimbursement decision is confirmed, but the corpus does not contain data on uptake rates, the number of patients expected to be treated, or the resulting demand for companion products. The causal chain from reimbursement to companion-product demand is plausible but unmeasured.
