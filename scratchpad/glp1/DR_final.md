## What the evidence does not support

- **Market size estimates for Europe are contradictory.** Mordor Intelligence projects the European GLP-1 agonist market at USD 4.17 billion in 2025 growing to USD 5.86 billion by 2031 at 5.84% CAGR [Europe GLP-1 Agonist Market - Share, Size & Industry Analysis](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/europe-glucagon-like-peptide-1-agonists-market), while another Mordor Intelligence report projects the European Insulin and GLP-1 Drugs market at USD 10.06 billion in 2025 growing to USD 12.93 billion by 2031 at 4.27% CAGR [Europe Insulin and GLP-1 Drugs Market - Share, Size & Industry Analysis](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/europe-insulin-drugs-and-glucagon-like-peptide-1-receptor-agaonist-market). The discrepancy likely reflects different scopes (GLP-1 only vs. insulin + GLP-1) and methodologies; neither figure should be used for investment sizing without reconciliation.

- **Food alone replicating GLP-1 satiety effects.** A May 2026 FoodNavigator article frames "satiety-enhancing foods" as a practical alternative to GLP-1 drugs [Satiety-Enhancing Foods: A Practical Alternative to GLP-1 Drugs](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/05/20/glp-1s-can-food-do-the-same-job-as-drugs/?utm_source=RSS_Feed&utm_medium=RSS&utm_campaign=RSS), while peer-reviewed evidence (PMC, 2026) shows that GLP-1 receptor blockade fails to suppress postprandial satiety or promote food intake in humans, and that the drug's effects are not replicable by dietary manipulation alone [Initial evidence that GLP-1 receptor blockade fails to suppress postprandial satiety or promote food intake in humans - PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4171349/). The consensus supported by primary evidence is that food can *support* but not *replace* GLP-1 therapy.

- **The "slow-moving" patent profile as a reason to avoid the space.** The measured median improvement rate and cycle time describe the core peptide chemistry (A61P5/48, C12N2501/335). This does not contradict the rapid expansion in clinical indications, oral formulations, and multi-agonist combinations, which are application-layer innovations not captured by the core patent citation graph. A company should not read the measured improvement rate as indicating a stagnant market; it indicates that the *molecule* science is maturing while the *use-case* frontier is accelerating.

- **Pediatric expansion as a near-term market driver.** Only one trial (semaglutide in children aged 6–12) is documented in the corpus (secondary source only); long-term safety data are limited and no regulatory approval for this age group has been confirmed in the evidence.
