## The question

A mid-sized storage integrator in the DACH region buys its lithium iron
phosphate (LFP) cells spot from China and sells containerised systems to
utilities and commercial customers. It has no cell manufacturing and does
not want any. Its board has to decide by the first quarter of 2027 whether
to commit up to €40 M to a multi-year supply of at least 500 MWh of cells a
year from a European or US producer — or keep buying spot.

That is a stylised case, not a client. We built it to test the question
that every European buyer of storage cells is facing this year, and we ran
it through our dossier-and-advisory chain: primary sources first, one
dossier on the LFP field, then an advisory note written for exactly this
buyer. What follows is the reasoning, with every figure linked to where it
comes from.

## What we found

The decision is not about chemistry. LFP has won stationary storage: about
90 % of the roughly 108 GW of battery storage added worldwide in 2025 was
LFP ([IEA, Global Energy Review 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-energy-review-2026/technology-battery-storage)).
A buyer of LFP cells is buying the default, not placing a bet.

The decision is about **origin, price and a date**.

- **The price floor is Chinese.** LFP cell prices fell from around $150/kWh
  in 2020 to about $100/kWh in 2024, with Chinese domestic cells below
  $60/kWh and BYD reported near $44/kWh ([market outlook](https://www.intelmarketresearch.com/lfp-energy-storage-cell-2025-2032-661-5950);
  [Battery Design on NMC vs LFP costs](https://www.batterydesign.net/nmc-vs-lfp-costs/)).
  Whoever buys outside China pays a premium for something other than the
  cell itself.
- **The date is 18 August 2027.** The EU Battery Regulation's due-diligence
  provisions, originally due in 2025, now apply from that day
  ([GreenSoft summary](https://www.greensofttech.com/blog-2025-eu-postpones-battery-due-diligence-obligations-to-2027/);
  [Battery Associates on Regulation 2023/1542](https://www.batteryregulation.eu/post/eu-battery-regulation)).
  The carbon-footprint declaration per battery model has applied since
  18 February 2025 for industrial batteries above 2 kWh (same source).
  Both land on a spot-bought Chinese cell just as they land on any other.
- **The supply side is moving under the buyer's feet.** China extended
  export controls to lithium-ion batteries and battery materials in
  October 2025 ([Benchmark](https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/china-extends-export-controls-to-lithium-ion-batteries-and-battery-materials);
  [ESS News](https://www.ess-news.com/2025/10/13/china-imposes-new-export-controls-on-lithium-ion-battery-technology/)).
  Europe's first large LFP line, the Stellantis–CATL plant in Zaragoza,
  targets production start at the end of 2026
  ([Business Insider DE](https://www.businessinsider.de/wirtschaft/stellantis-und-chinesische-firma-catl-wollen-batteriefabrik-in-europa-bauen/)),
  and ElevenEs is building a 1 GWh LFP cell factory in Serbia
  ([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/caterpillar-vc-backs-startup-elevenes-as-it-builds-1gwh-lfp-cell-factory-in-serbia/)).

## The evidence, option by option

The advisory note laid out three executable paths for this buyer. The
fourth thing people usually propose — co-funding a cell line — is out of
scope for a company without electrochemistry.

| Option | What has to be true | What kills it |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Multi-year supply from a European producer** (Zaragoza, ElevenEs) | A European line is actually producing: Zaragoza targets end-2026 ([source](https://www.businessinsider.de/wirtschaft/stellantis-und-chinesische-firma-catl-wollen-batteriefabrik-in-europa-bauen/)); the contract is signed by Q1 2027 so deliveries land before 18 August 2027 | No firm 500 MWh/yr allocation confirmed by Q4 2026 |
| **2 — Multi-year supply from a US producer** (Spring Hill, SK On, Ford) | A US producer is willing to ship to Europe at a landed price inside the buyer's tender economics | Every US commitment stays with US offtakers — see below |
| **3 — Keep buying Chinese spot** (the null option) | No due-diligence obligation in force yet, Chinese cells at the $44–60/kWh floor | 18 August 2027, or an export-control measure that blocks a scheduled shipment |

**Why the US route looks weaker than it sounds.** The US LFP storage
capacity in the record is real — Ultium Cells (GM/LG Energy Solution)
begins LFP storage-cell production at Spring Hill, Tennessee in Q2 2026
([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/ultium-cells-70-million-investment-to-shift-production-toward-energy-storage-systems/);
[pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/18/lg-and-gm-pivot-ultium-cells-jv-to-lfp-battery-production-for-u-s-storage-market-at-tennessee-plant/)),
SK On has a 9 GWh LFP pouch-cell supply agreement with NeoVolta Power for
2027–2031 ([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/sk-on-signs-18gwh-reciprocal-lfp-supply-deal-with-neovolta-power-for-us-made-battery-storage/)),
and Ford Energy's first stationary-storage deliveries are due in late 2027
([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/ford-officially-launches-us-stationary-energy-storage-subsidiary-deliveries-to-begin-in-2027/)).
But every one of those commitments is contracted to a US buyer or built for
the US market, and the US is heading into an oversupply of storage cells
([Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/01/almost-overnight-the-us-became-an-oversupply-market-for-ess-battery-cells/)).
The record contains no case of a US producer shipping LFP storage cells to
Europe. A European integrator would be the first and least important
customer on a supply chain built for another continent — and would carry
transatlantic freight on top, while the US–China tariff suspension only
runs to 10 November 2026 ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/11/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-strikes-deal-on-economic-and-trade-relations-with-china/)).

**What the commitment is worth.** The record holds no contract value for a
comparable buyer's deal, so the honest sizing is arithmetic: 500 MWh a year
at $44–100/kWh is a cell stream of roughly $22–50 M a year. A €40 M
multi-year commitment is therefore a prepayment or take-or-pay tranche on
that stream, not the stream itself. Anyone who tells you the "effort" of
such a contract from a grant ceiling or a headline plant investment is
measuring the wrong thing.

**What the evidence does not tell you.** Zaragoza's capacity in GWh is not
in the record — only the €4.1 B investment and the end-2026 start. Whether
a 500 MWh/yr allocation is available to a mid-sized buyer is exactly the
question the buyer has to put to the producer. And the EU's own LFP demand
is small: the one estimate in the record puts it at about a tenth of global
demand, almost entirely imported ([fleet guide](https://www.evinfrastructurenews.com/ev-battery/lfp-vs-nmc-battery-comparison-fleet-guide)).
A European cell is a scarce, premium input, not a like-for-like swap.

## What this means

For this buyer the advisory note recommends **Option 1 with medium
confidence**: secure a multi-year European LFP supply of at least 500 MWh a
year, structured with a volume floor and ceiling and a slip remedy rather
than a blind take-or-pay, signed by Q1 2027. It is the only option that
answers the question as the board posed it — European volume, inside the
€40 M envelope, before the regulatory date — and the only one that also
clears the compliance wall the buyer's Chinese spot cells will hit.

The null option is not a strategy. It keeps the cheapest cells today and
inherits two dated, non-negotiable shocks — EU due diligence on 18 August
2027 and China's battery export controls — from a spot position it cannot
hedge. That is precisely the price-taker exposure the board was worried
about.

Two facts would change the read. If neither Zaragoza nor ElevenEs confirms
a firm allocation by Q4 2026, the fallback is a smaller European anchor
plus a hedged Chinese spot book, not a single European line. And if a US
producer offers a landed European price inside the buyer's tender economics
by Q1 2027 — against the pattern of the whole record — the US route reopens
as a second source to cut single-supplier concentration.

What the medium confidence is telling you: the regulatory date is firm, the
price floor is firm, but the European supply that would let a buyer act on
both is still a plan with a start date. The decision is less "which
chemistry" than "who will sell you the one that already won".
