## The question

Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) was the cheap, heavy, "good enough" chemistry.
Is it now the default — and if so, what does a European buyer or maker of
stationary storage actually have to decide before 2027?

## What we found

LFP is not a promise waiting to be validated. It has displaced nickel-based
chemistries in grid storage worldwide and in most of China's EV market. The
open question for Europe is not *whether* but *who supplies it*: demand is
here, cells are not.

- In 2025 the world added roughly 108 GW of battery storage, and about 90 %
  of it was LFP ([IEA, Global Energy Review 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-energy-review-2026/technology-battery-storage)).
- In China, LFP took 80 % of vehicle battery installations from January to
  April 2026 — 149.8 GWh against 37.4 GWh for ternary chemistries (China
  Automotive Power Battery Innovation Alliance, via the 2026 battery
  technology review cited in the dossier).
- The price gap is now structural: average LFP packs at $81/kWh against
  $128/kWh for NMC in BloombergNEF's 2025 survey ([BNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-transport/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour-despite-rising-metal-prices-bloombergnef/)).
- Europe's own LFP capacity is still a plan: the Stellantis–CATL plant in
  Zaragoza targets production start at the end of 2026. In the United States
  the pivot is already under way — Ultium Cells (GM/LGES) retooling Spring Hill
  for LFP storage cells, SK On's 9 GWh LFP supply deal with NeoVolta for
  2027–2031, Ford Energy's first stationary-storage deliveries in late 2027.

is a dense layer of secondary patents on coatings, doping, LMFP and
cell-to-pack designs, which LG Energy Solution said in April 2024 it would
enforce. The dossier's own measurement on CPC class H01M4/5825 (phosphate
cathodes, 16,047 patents, 12,145 in the citation graph) is in the appendix.

## What this means

[Deine Einordnung. Aus dem Dossier tragen drei Punkte: (1) LFP ist ein
Rohstoff-Markt geworden — die Marge liegt in Integration und Lieferkette,
nicht in der Zelle; (2) Natrium-Ionen (CATL Naxtra in Serie, 60 GWh mit
HyperStrong) und LMFP sind Ergänzungen, keine Ablösung vor 2030; (3) die
Eisen-Luft-Batterie ist ein anderes Segment (Langzeitspeicher), kein
LFP-Konkurrent — das Dossier führt sie bewusst unter „What the evidence does
not support".]

What the evidence does *not* say: that LFP cell prices are uniformly
~$100/kWh (the spread runs from $36/kWh lowest observed to $108/kWh average
pack — different measurement bases), or that the EU market has adopted LFP
domestically (roughly 10 % of demand in 2025, almost entirely imported).
